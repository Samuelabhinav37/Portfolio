Every public IP on the internet gets scanned within minutes of coming online. Instead of treating that as noise, a honeypot turns it into a personal threat intelligence feed: fresh IOCs, live malware samples, and a ground-truth view of what automated attack traffic actually looks like.

## The stack

A single t3.medium running T-Pot covers a surprising amount of ground: Cowrie for SSH/Telnet, Dionaea for SMB and databases, and an ELK front end that makes 30 days of attack data explorable in an afternoon.

## What the data taught me

Credential stuffing lists are older than you think, Mirai variants never died, and the gap between a CVE dropping and mass exploitation attempts hitting the honeypot is now measured in hours, not days.
